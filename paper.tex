\begin{abstract}
We calculate the redshift-drift response to a radially varying bang time in Lema\^itre--Tolman--Bondi (LTB) models by propagating neighboring light cones. An exact parabolic dust solution yields an explicit variational equation and reveals that the initially studied hump-shaped bang-time profiles have shell crossings near the bang for either sign of their amplitude. Replacing them with monotone profiles gives a globally shell-crossing-free expanding parabolic family for positive amplitude. Its equal-distance drift response is positive at all 15 sampled redshifts $0.1\leq z\leq1.5$, opposite to the diagnostic hump profiles. An exact flat $\Lambda$-LTB family with $(\Omega_m,\Omega_\Lambda)=(0.3,0.7)$ gives the same sampled sign. We also derive a linear curvature compensation that enforces $\delta D_L(z)=0$ locally; its center-regular solution grows sharply toward the background angular-distance maximum, where the inverse equation is singular. These are model-family results, not a measurement or a universal sign theorem. The numerical integrations, independent derivative checks, and figures are reproducible.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and relation to earlier work}
Redshift drift in spherically symmetric spacetimes and its use with distance data were established by Uzan, Clarkson and Ellis~\cite{uzan}. Yoo, Kai and Nakao developed the LTB drift calculation and studied void models~\cite{yoo}. Exact growing and decaying density modes in LTB dust have been analyzed by Sussman~\cite{sussman}. The inverse problem for LTB observables has additional existence and regularity conditions~\cite{inverse}. We therefore claim neither a new general drift equation nor a unique observational signature. The purpose here is to correct an endpoint-formula error in an earlier unpublished draft, spell out an explicit parameter derivative, and give a small reproducible test case.

In units $c=G=1$, the LTB metric and evolution equation are
\begin{align}
\dd s^2&=-\dd t^2+\frac{R'(t,r)^2}{1+2E(r)}\dd r^2+R(t,r)^2\dd\Omega^2,\label{eq:metric}\\
\dot R^2&=\frac{2M(r)}{R}+2E(r),\qquad R(\tb(r),r)=0.\label{eq:field}
\end{align}
Dots and primes denote partial derivatives at fixed $r$ and $t$, respectively. A radial coordinate choice removes one of $M,E,\tb$ as an independent function. A nonconstant $\tb$ represents the decaying mode in the conventional LTB dust mode decomposition~\cite{sussman}; its additive constant sets the time origin. Throughout this paper the mass-coordinate gauge and the profiles below are specified explicitly.

The calculation asks five concrete questions: how to propagate the drift of the same source; how comparison at fixed redshift differs from equal distance; whether the response's sign survives changes of bang-time shape and regularity conditions; whether a curvature mode can keep the full distance curve fixed; and whether these effects persist on a matter-plus-$\Lambda$ background. The equations and computed results below answer these questions within precisely stated model families. Observational viability requires a separate fit to early- and late-time data.

\section{Drift on neighboring radial light cones}
Put $S(r)=\sqrt{1+2E(r)}$, $A=R'/S$, $B=\dot R'/S$, and $C=\ddot R'/S$. On an incoming radial ray from a central, comoving observer at $r=0$, the emission time $t(r)$ and observed redshift $z(r)$ satisfy
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd t}{\dd r}=-A,\qquad \frac{\dd z}{\dd r}=(1+z)B,\qquad t(0)=t_0,\quad z(0)=0.\label{eq:ray}
\end{equation}
Compare two rays received at times separated by $\delta t_0$, from the \emph{same comoving emitter} $r$. Their time separation $T(r)=\partial t(r;t_0)/\partial t_0$ obeys $T'=-BT$, $T(0)=1$, hence $T=1/(1+z)$. Let $Z(r)=\partial z(r;t_0)/\partial t_0$ be drift per unit central proper time. Differentiating Eq.~\eqref{eq:ray} with respect to $t_0$ at fixed $r$ gives the exact propagation equation
\begin{equation}
Z'=BZ+(1+z)CT=BZ+C,\qquad Z(0)=0,\label{eq:drift}
\end{equation}
or, equivalently,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\quad Z(r)=(1+z(r))\int_0^r\frac{C(\bar r)}{1+z(\bar r)}\,\dd\bar r.\quad}\label{eq:integral}
\end{equation}
This result carries the information along the ray. In a homogeneous FLRW limit it reduces to $Z=(1+z)H_0-H(t_e)$. In a general LTB geometry, replacing Eq.~\eqref{eq:drift} by $(1+z)H_0-\dot R'/R'|_e$ is not justified: the gradient of expansion along the ray contributes. For an isotropic central observer, reciprocity gives
\begin{equation}
\D(r)=(1+z(r))^2R(t(r),r).\label{eq:distance}
\end{equation}

\section{Linear response and what ``fixed distance'' means}
Let $p$ denote a model parameter and $\bm y=(t,z,Z)$. With $\bm y'=\bm F(\bm y,r,p)$ from Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ray} and \eqref{eq:drift}, its first-order variation $\bm u=\partial_p\bm y|_r$ is specified, without finite differences, by
\begin{equation}
\bm u'=\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}
-A_t&0&0\\(1+z)B_t&B&0\\ B_tZ+C_t&0&B
\end{pmatrix}}_{\bm F_{\bm y}}\bm u+
\begin{pmatrix}-A_p\\(1+z)B_p\\B_pZ+C_p\end{pmatrix},\qquad \bm u(0)=\bm0.\label{eq:sensitivity}
\end{equation}
All partial derivatives in this equation are at fixed $t,r$; in particular, $A_p$ includes any parameter dependence in $S$. Equation~\eqref{eq:sensitivity} is the analytic variational equation used to define the linear response; the public numerical example evaluates its derivative by symmetric finite differences and checks step convergence.

At a given emitter coordinate the distance response is
\begin{equation}
\partial_p\D|_r=(1+z)^2(R_t t_p+R_p)+2(1+z)Rz_p,
\qquad
\frac{\dd\D}{\dd r}=2(1+z)z_rR+(1+z)^2(R_t t_r+R_r).\label{eq:Dderivs}
\end{equation}
Here $R_p$ and $R_r$ are partial derivatives, whereas $\dd\D/\dd r$ is a total derivative along the ray. If $\dd\D/\dd r\ne0$, implicit differentiation yields the response at a \emph{single equal luminosity distance},
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\left.\frac{\partial Z}{\partial p}\right|_{\D}=Z_p|_r-\frac{Z_r}{\dd\D/\dd r}\,\partial_p\D|_r.}\label{eq:fixedD}
\end{equation}
For comparison, $\partial_p Z|_z=Z_p|_r-(Z_r/z_r)z_p|_r$. Neither condition requires $\D(z)$ to be identical between the two models. Keeping the \emph{whole function} $\D(z)$ fixed would require a compensating functional perturbation in $M$ or $E$ and is a distinct inverse problem. Equations~\eqref{eq:sensitivity}--\eqref{eq:fixedD} also show that a zero-crossing location cannot be inferred from the background profile alone without specifying the allowed perturbation and compensation.

\section{Exactly specified illustrative model}
Set $t_0=1$, $E(r)=0$, $M(r)=2r^3/9$, and $\tb(r)=\epsilon f_w(r)$, with
\begin{equation}
f_w(r)=r^2\exp[-(r/w)^2],\qquad w\in\{0.8,1.2\}.\label{eq:profile}
\end{equation}
All coordinates and drift values in this section are dimensionless. The two choices of $w$ are perturbation shapes on the \emph{same homogeneous background}, not two realistic void profiles. The even behavior at $r=0$ makes the center smooth. The exact expanding solution, with $\tau=t-\epsilon f_w(r)$, is
\begin{align}
R&=r\tau^{2/3},\nonumber\\
R'&=\tau^{2/3}-\frac{2r\epsilon f'_w}{3\tau^{1/3}},\quad
\dot R'=\frac{2}{3\tau^{1/3}}+\frac{2r\epsilon f'_w}{9\tau^{4/3}},\label{eq:exact}\\
\ddot R'&=-\frac{2}{9\tau^{4/3}}-\frac{8r\epsilon f'_w}{27\tau^{7/3}}.\nonumber
\end{align}
The expansion branch requires $\tau>0$; the code checks $R'>0$ and monotonic $z,\D$ on every sampled light cone. At $\epsilon=0$, the model is Einstein--de Sitter (EdS) and has the exact check
\begin{equation}
Z_{\rm EdS}(z)=\frac23\big[(1+z)-(1+z)^{3/2}\big].\label{eq:eds}
\end{equation}
At $\epsilon\ne0$ we hold $M,E,t_0$ fixed, integrate to $z=1.6$, and compare drift values at the \emph{baseline} $\D(z)$ for $z=0.1,0.2,\ldots,1.5$. The derivative uses $[Z(+h)-Z(-h)]/(2h)$ at $h=0.002,0.001,0.0005$. The plotted result uses $h=0.0005$.

At $\epsilon=0$, the full null-cone solution provides further independent checks:
\begin{equation}
t(r)=(1-r/3)^3,\qquad 1+z(r)=(1-r/3)^{-2},\qquad
\D(z)=3\big[(1+z)-\sqrt{1+z}\big].\label{eq:edscone}
\end{equation}
The source radius corresponding to a baseline redshift is $r(z)=3[1-(1+z)^{-1/2}]$. It is this baseline distance and radius, rather than the perturbed model's redshift label, that define the equal-distance sample.

\section{Global shell-crossing test and a regular bang-time family}
For the parabolic solution of Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact}, $R'=\tau^{-1/3}[\tau-2r\tb'(r)/3]$. For an expanding shell with $r>0$, $\tb'>0$ therefore forces $R'=0$ at $\tau=2r\tb'/3>0$, a shell crossing after the local bang. The diagnostic hump $f_w=r^2e^{-(r/w)^2}$ has $f'_w>0$ for $0<r<w$ and $f'_w<0$ for $r>w$. Thus \emph{either nonzero sign} of $\epsilon$ produces shell crossings somewhere in the unbounded model. The negative curves in Figs.~\ref{fig:response} and \ref{fig:comparisons} are valid computations on their sampled light cones but are not globally regular cosmologies.

Consider instead the centrally smooth, monotone family
\begin{equation}
\tb(r)=\eta f_w^-(r),\qquad f_w^-(r)=-[1-e^{-(r/w)^2}],
\qquad \eta\geq0,\quad w\in\{0.8,1.2\}.\label{eq:monotone}
\end{equation}
Here $\tb'\leq0$, hence $R'=\tau^{2/3}-2r\tb'/(3\tau^{1/3})>0$ at every $r>0$ and $\tau>0$. This proves absence of shell crossings in the expanding parabolic solution, though it does not establish observational viability. The exact variational equation yields a \emph{positive} equal-distance derivative at all 15 sampled redshifts: $[0.002324,2.152592]$ for $w=0.8$ and $[0.001042,1.546575]$ for $w=1.2$. Richardson extrapolation from $\eta=0.001$ and $0.0005$ agrees with the variational result within $2.93\times10^{-7}$ and $1.36\times10^{-7}$, respectively. The original negative response is therefore not stable under an admissible change of perturbation family; neither family has a sampled zero crossing.

\section{An exact matter-plus-\texorpdfstring{$\Lambda$}{Lambda} background}
The sign check is repeated in a flat $\Lambda$-LTB subclass with $H_0=1$, $\Omega_m=0.3$, $\Omega_\Lambda=0.7$, $M(r)=\Omega_m r^3/2$, $E=0$, and $\Lambda=3\Omega_\Lambda$. Its exact expanding solution is $R(t,r)=r a(t-\tb(r))$, where
\begin{equation}
a(\tau)=\left(\frac{\Omega_m}{\Omega_\Lambda}\right)^{1/3}
\sinh^{2/3}\!\left(\frac32\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}\,\tau\right),\quad
t_0=\frac{2\operatorname{arsinh}\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda/\Omega_m}}{3\sqrt{\Omega_\Lambda}}=0.964099\ldots .\label{eq:lambdaa}
\end{equation}
For Eq.~\eqref{eq:monotone}, $R'=a-r\tb'\dot a>0$ for every expanding post-bang shell. We use $\dot R'=\dot a-r\tb'\ddot a$ and $\ddot R'=\ddot a-r\tb'\dddot a$ in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:ray} and \eqref{eq:drift}. The homogeneous limit reproduces
\begin{equation}
\frac{Z_{\Lambda\mathrm{CDM}}(z)}{H_0}=(1+z)-\sqrt{0.3(1+z)^3+0.7}.\label{eq:lambdacheck}
\end{equation}
The maximum absolute error against Eq.~\eqref{eq:lambdacheck} is $2.85\times10^{-11}$ on the grid; neighboring-ray differencing agrees with propagated drift within $3.40\times10^{-11}$. The equal-distance responses remain positive at all 15 points, ranging from $0.000826$ to $2.127014$ ($w=0.8$) and $0.000369$ to $1.440526$ ($w=1.2$), in units of $\partial_\eta(Z/H_0)$. The two-step positive-amplitude extrapolations differ by at most $2.84\times10^{-6}$ and $6.55\times10^{-7}$. Figure~\ref{fig:lambda} shows the calculation. This calibrated \emph{background} is not a fitted inhomogeneous void.

\section{Functional distance compensation in the dust model}
To answer the stronger fixed-distance-\emph{curve} question, set $2E(r)=\eta r^2e(r)$ while holding $M=2r^3/9$ and $\tb=\eta f(r)$. To first order about EdS, the exact evolution equation gives
\begin{equation}
R(t,r)=r\left[t^{2/3}+\eta\left(\frac9{20}e(r)t^{4/3}-\frac23 f(r)t^{-1/3}\right)\right]+O(\eta^2).\label{eq:growing}
\end{equation}
This makes the curvature (growing) and bang-time (decaying) variations explicit. Write $u_t=\partial_\eta t|_r$, $u_z=\partial_\eta z|_r$, and $q=1-r/3$ on the EdS null cone. Direct substitution into $\delta D_L|_z=0$ reduces the entire distance constraint to
\begin{equation}
Q(r)\equiv\frac{2r}{3q^5}[u_t-f(r)]+\frac32(r-1)u_z+\frac{9r}{20}e(r)=0.\label{eq:Q}
\end{equation}
At every $0<r<1$ this equation algebraically determines $e$ in terms of the ray variations. Substituting it and its derivative into the variational light-cone equations yields a local initial-value problem. Its coefficient multiplying the solved-for $e'$ is $(r-1)/q$; hence the inversion is singular at $r=1$, where the EdS angular-diameter distance has its maximum ($z=1.25$). A smooth continuation requires an additional compatibility condition there, consistent with known LTB inverse-problem regularity issues~\cite{inverse}.

For the monotone profiles, a center-regular numerical integration to $r=0.98$ enforces $|Q|<6\times10^{-14}$ on the evaluated grid, but the required $e(r)$ grows from $32.58$ at $r=0.9$ to $657.80$ at $r=0.98$ for $w=0.8$ (and $18.59$ to $376.28$ for $w=1.2$). Moving the numerical start radius from $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-6}$ changes the values at $r=0.9$ by less than $2\times10^{-3}$. Figure~\ref{fig:constrained} shows this rapid growth and the associated drift response. Thus a \emph{local} first-order distance compensation exists, but these center-regular solutions do not yield a controlled smooth compensation through the distance maximum in this fixed-$M$ gauge. Near that point $|\eta e|\ll1$ fails for any fixed nonzero $\eta$, so the first-order curve cannot be promoted to a finite-amplitude prediction there. We do not claim a general impossibility theorem for other bang-time functions or gauges.

\section{Answers and observational limits}
Within the stated families, the source drift is Eq.~\eqref{eq:drift}, and the fixed-$z$ and equal-$D_L$ responses differ as Eq.~\eqref{eq:fixedD} and Fig.~\ref{fig:comparisons} demonstrate. The diagnostic hump profiles have shell crossings and a negative sampled response; monotone shell-crossing-free profiles give a positive sampled response in both EdS and flat $\Lambda$ backgrounds. No profile-only universal zero crossing follows. Requiring the \emph{whole} distance curve to agree is more restrictive than matching one distance: the explicitly reconstructed curvature mode develops a singular inverse problem near the angular-distance maximum for our profiles.

Global absence of shell crossing does not imply consistency with the CMB, kSZ effect, supernovae, or structure formation. Earlier analyses of general varying-bang-time voids found strong combined constraints~\cite{bull}; significant decaying modes in void models that mimic acceleration are also constrained by CMB spectral distortions~\cite{zibin}. Those results do not supply a numerical bound for the $\Lambda$-LTB profiles specified here without fitting them. Our background calibration is not a data likelihood, and the dimensionless derivatives are not a forecast for a telescope. The concrete conclusion is a reproducible, profile-dependent response and a demonstrated inversion obstruction in the tested gauge, not a detected signal.

\section{Explicit forcing of the bang-time variation}\label{sec:forcing}
For the profile of Eq.~\eqref{eq:profile}, define $g(r)=f_w(r)+r f'_w(r)$. Holding $t,r$ fixed and differentiating Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact} at $\epsilon=0$ gives
\begin{equation}
A_\epsilon=-\frac{2g}{3t^{1/3}},\qquad
B_\epsilon=\frac{2g}{9t^{4/3}},\qquad
C_\epsilon=-\frac{8g}{27t^{7/3}}.\label{eq:forcing}
\end{equation}
The baseline time derivatives needed in Eq.~\eqref{eq:sensitivity} are
\begin{equation}
A_t=\frac{2}{3t^{1/3}},\qquad
B_t=-\frac{2}{9t^{4/3}},\qquad
C_t=\frac{8}{27t^{7/3}}.\label{eq:timeforcing}
\end{equation}
We integrate Eq.~\eqref{eq:sensitivity} with these forcing terms along Eq.~\eqref{eq:edscone}, then apply Eqs.~\eqref{eq:Dderivs} and \eqref{eq:fixedD}. This analytically differentiated initial-value problem provides a check independent of changing $\epsilon$ in the nonlinear ray integrator. Agreement on the sampled interval tests the implementation; it does not guarantee convergence at arbitrarily high redshift or global absence of shell crossings.

\section{Coefficient of the distance-inversion equation}
Solving Eq.~\eqref{eq:Q} for the curvature perturbation gives
\begin{equation}
e=-\frac{40}{27q^5}(u_t-f)-\frac{10(r-1)}{3r}u_z.\label{eq:solvee}
\end{equation}
At fixed $r$, its derivatives with respect to the ray variations are $e_{u_t}=-40/(27q^5)$ and $e_{u_z}=-10(r-1)/(3r)$. In the first two variational equations, the coefficients of $e'$ are respectively $G_t=-9rq^4/20$ and $G_z=3r/(5q)$. Implicit differentiation of Eq.~\eqref{eq:solvee} therefore puts the coefficient
\begin{equation}
1-e_{u_t}G_t-e_{u_z}G_z=\frac{r-1}{q}\label{eq:singular}
\end{equation}
in front of $e'$. This vanishes at the EdS angular-distance maximum. The reconstruction script evaluates the full right-hand side before division and records the resulting local solution; it does not numerically step across $r=1$.
